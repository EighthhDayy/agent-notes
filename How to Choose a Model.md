# How to Choose a Model

what kind of model should be chosen to power an Agent?

The model is the foundation of an Agent's intelligence. **Choosing the right model is often more effective than optimizing prompts.** Due to the rapid evolution of models, this section does not recommend specific model versions, but instead provides some guidance on model selection.

**Closed-Source Models.** Currently, the two most commonly used closed-source model providers in Agent development are OpenAI (GPT/o series) and Anthropic (Claude series). Closed-source models generally lead in capability, but they are more expensive and subject to the providers' API policies. When selecting a model, do not just look at leaderboards; **evaluate it on your own tasks** (see Chapter 7).

**Open-Source Models.** At the time of writing, the gap between open-source and closed-source models is within six months, while the cost of open-source models is significantly lower. If your business scenario does not require advanced model capabilities, open-source models are a practical choice. Open-source models are low-cost, can be privately deployed, and support customized fine-tuning, making them suitable for scenarios that are cost-sensitive or have data compliance requirements. DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM are Chinese models with strong Agent capabilities. It is worth noting that different models vary greatly in their tool-calling capabilities, so they must be tested in specific scenarios before selection.

**Beyond capabilities, the model's policy boundaries must also be considered.** A model demonstrating a certain capability in a benchmark does not necessarily mean that the product hosting it allows users to invoke that capability. Different providers set different policy boundaries for cybersecurity, model distillation, model extraction, private data, and high-risk operations. The same task may also produce different results across chat products, Coding Agents, and APIs. Therefore, model selection should not be based solely on accuracy, price, and speed. Models must also be tested on actual tasks: whether the model is willing to execute them, whether the interface exposes the required capabilities, and whether the terms of service permit such usage. For business-critical tasks, human intervention or other compliant models should also be prepared in advance as alternative paths.

**The vast majority of Agents require models that support reasoning.** Agents need to make complex decisions involving multi-step reasoning and tool selection. Models without reasoning capabilities often perform poorly on these tasks. There are only a few exceptions, such as executing simple single-step tasks or performing simple GUI operations in Computer Use that only require clicking a fixed position. In these cases, models without reasoning capabilities can also handle the tasks. However, whenever multi-step reasoning or dynamic decision-making is involved, a model that supports reasoning must be selected.

**Pay attention to output speed and multimodal capabilities.** Beyond cost, there are two dimensions that are easily overlooked. The first is **token output speed**: Agents often require multiple rounds of inference, and each round must wait for the model to complete its output before proceeding to the next step. Therefore, output speed directly determines end-to-end response latency—if an Agent task requires 20 rounds of inference, an additional two seconds per round means an additional 40 seconds of waiting in total. The second is **multimodal support**: if your Agent needs to understand images, audio, or video, multimodal capabilities are a mandatory requirement, and different models vary greatly in this regard.
